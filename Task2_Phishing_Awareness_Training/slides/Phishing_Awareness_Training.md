# Phishing Awareness Training
**CodeAlpha Cyber Security Internship — Maniraj Duvvaka**

## 1. What is phishing?
A social-engineering attack that attempts to make a person reveal information, open harmful content, transfer money, or take another unsafe action.

## 2. Common phishing types
- Email phishing
- Spear phishing
- Business email compromise
- Smishing (SMS)
- Vishing (voice)
- Clone phishing
- Fake login pages

## 3. How attackers create pressure
- Urgency: “Act now”
- Authority: impersonating a manager or service
- Fear: account suspension or legal threats
- Curiosity: unexpected document or photo
- Reward: refund, prize or job offer

## 4. Inspect the sender
Check the complete sender address, spelling, domain and context. A familiar display name does not prove authenticity.

## 5. Inspect links safely
Hover before clicking when possible. Look for unexpected domains, misspellings, shortened URLs and suspicious subdomains. When uncertain, navigate to the official site yourself.

## 6. Fake websites
A polished design and HTTPS do not prove legitimacy. Verify the domain independently before entering credentials.

## 7. Attachments and QR codes
Treat unexpected attachments and QR codes as untrusted. Verify the request through a known contact method.

## 8. Verification rule
**STOP → use a trusted channel → verify → then act.**

## 9. If you clicked
Do not hide the incident. Report promptly, follow your organization's incident-response process and change credentials through the legitimate service if instructed.

## 10. Mini quiz
1. Does HTTPS guarantee a legitimate site?
2. What is smishing?
3. What is the safest response to an unexpected password-reset message?
4. Why is urgency a warning sign?

## 11. Best practices
- Use MFA
- Use a password manager
- Keep software updated
- Verify unusual payment/credential requests
- Report suspicious messages
- Never share OTPs or recovery codes

## 12. Key takeaway
**Pause → Inspect → Verify → Report**
